# The Code Book — Chapter 1 Questions

## When a Message Itself Becomes Dangerous

> Imagine you are carrying a letter that could change the fate of a kingdom. The letter contains no weapons, no soldiers, and no money—only words. Yet if the wrong person reads those words, people could be arrested or killed. What would be more important: finding a way to hide the letter, or finding a way to make the letter unreadable to anyone who finds it?

## Steganography

> You need to send a secret message through a city where guards inspect every letter. You cannot send an obviously coded message because that itself would make the guards suspicious. Instead, you find a way to make the secret message look like something completely ordinary. What would you rather have: a message nobody notices, or a message everyone can see but nobody can understand?

## Greece and Persia

> Imagine your enemy controls the roads, watches the borders, and checks the messages being carried through the kingdom. You discover that an attack is coming, but simply sending a warning could expose both the messenger and the people who helped you. How could you warn your people without allowing the enemy to realize that a warning had even been sent? And what might happen if that secret warning arrives in time?
