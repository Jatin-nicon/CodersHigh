# Day 1 - Page 51 to 55

## Questions

Q1.)  How is the Vigenere cipher similar to a computer program?Can we identify its inputs,key,processing rules and output?

Q2.)  Babbage's Analytical Engine included memory,a processor and the ability to repeat instructions.How are these ideas connected to modern computers?Which features of today's computers reflect his original vision?

Q3.) If you were designing a secure messaging application today,what lessons would you take from the Vigenere cipher and Babbage's work?How would you balance security,speed,reliability and ease of use?

Q4.) Why do you think Babbage struggled to complete his Difference Engine despite having brilliant ideas?What does his experience teach us about the difference between designing a technology and actually building it?

Q5.) How did the invention of the telegraph change the importance of encryption?Why did businesses need to protect messages when another person was responsible for transmitting them?

Q6.) Why do humans naturally look for patterns when solving problems?How can this instinct help a codebreaker and can it sometimes lead them to the wrong conclusion?

Q7.) Can something that looks random actually follow a fixed pattern?The Vigenere cipher uses a repeating keyword.How can a system appear confusing to an outsider while following simple rules internally?

Q8.) Imagine you see the letter Z appearing many times in a secret message. Would you immediately assume it stands for the letter E?What could go wrong if you trust your first guess?

Q9.) Suppose you create your own encryption method and your friends cannot break it.How would you know whether your method is genuinely secure or whether your friends just haven't found its weakness yet?

Q10.) The Vigenere cipher repeats its keyword after a fixed number of letters.How could a programmer use this repeating pattern to encrypt a message?What programming concepts would be useful,such as loops,arrays and modular arithmetic?
