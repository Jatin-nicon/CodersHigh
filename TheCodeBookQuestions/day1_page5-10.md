## Questions

Q1.) How is the blood relationship between Mary Queen of Scots, and Queen Elizabeth I portrayed within the social and political context of Elizabethan society?

Q2.) What was Mary, Queen of Scots, accused of, and what was the objective of the alleged plot?

Q3.) Who was Sir Francis Walsingham? His role in the investigation and what actions he took?

Q4.) Why did written/intercepted communication become important in solving a secret conspiracy case?

Q5.) From the point of view of cryptography, identify two approaches that have interest in secret communication.

Q6.) Suppose the investigators obtained a message containing symbols that does not reveal its meaning to its conspirators. What computational problem would this create?

Q7.) If you were to design an algorithm to investigate secret messages, what information would you collect before decoding them?

Q8.) Why is it useful to identify the background of people involved in the communication process before attempting to decode a cipher?

Q9.) Is a ciphertext similar to plaintext? Explain the difference between them.

Q10.) What transformation does a cipher perform on a message?

Q11.) Does knowing a message was encrypted necessarily mean that it is secure? Explain with the example of Mary Queen.

Q12.) What’s the difference between decrypting a message using the correct key and breaking the cipher without being given the key?

Q13.) Imagine you are Walsingham and have only ciphertext. List the first three steps to recover the original. What would be the input and output of the cryptanalysis algorithm?

Q14.) Consider the chain:
Mary → plaintext → encryption → ciphertext → Walsingham → cryptanalysis → plaintext

At which stage does the main security mechanism operate, and at what stage is Walsingham trying to defeat it?

Q15.) Did Demaratus conspirators encrypt the message, or did he hide the existence of the message? Explain.
